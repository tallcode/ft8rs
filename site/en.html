About

ft8.rs decodes FT8 signals. Feed audio from your radio or SDR into your computer to see received callsigns, signal levels and messages.

It is receive-only, with no transmit function. Listen to activity across the band or follow a particular callsign. Four decoding modes are available: WSJT-X, JTDX, Hybrid and DX.

Getting started

These steps are for the Windows graphical app.

  1. Connect your audio. Tune your radio or SDR to the FT8 frequency you want to receive. Route its audio to your computer using a soundcard, USB audio or a virtual audio cable. Keep your computer's clock accurate.
  2. Open the app. Extract the entire ZIP archive and run ft8rs-gui.exe.
  3. Select the input. In Settings → Audio, choose your receiver's input device and channel. Check that an audio level is shown. If the signal is present on only one channel, choose Left or Right as appropriate.
  4. Choose a mode. Start with the default WSJT-X mode. You can try other modes in Settings → Decode.
  5. Start listening. Click the play button in the main window. FT8 reception follows 15-second slots; decoded messages appear as they become available after each slot. Click stop to finish.

You do not need to enter your own callsign for general listening. If needed, enter your callsign and grid locator in Settings → Station.

Choosing a decoding mode

WSJT-X
The default mode, suitable for getting started.
JTDX
Uses JTDX decoding. Try it alongside the default mode.
Hybrid
Combines WSJT-X and JTDX results and uses more computing resources.
DX
Follows a target callsign. Enter it in the main window's Callsign field before listening. You can also double-click a decoded message to select a target.

No messages?

Check the receive frequency, computer clock and audio input first. In Settings → Audio, check that the receiver's audio is selected and the level is not overloaded. If the level never changes, check the device, channel and your system's audio input permissions.

Linux command line

Open a terminal in the extracted directory. List the input devices, then start listening with the appropriate device number:

./ft8rs monitor
./ft8rs monitor --device 0

The command-line tool also decodes WAV recordings. See the full user guide for more audio settings, recording playback and UDP options.
